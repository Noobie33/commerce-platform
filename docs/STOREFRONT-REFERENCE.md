# Storefront reference and feature contract

Owner direction recorded on 8 November 2026: use Aarong/Taaga as the primary reference for storefront composition, product presentation and shopping functionality; add Tabaya-inspired product video alongside photographs. Apply the shared clarity and visual language to staff screens, and extend the management system to supply all storefront content. Neutral code branding remains in force.

This direction extends the accepted requirements. It does not mean the public store or every reference feature is already implemented. The working system remains the staff ERP foundation described in the README.

## Confirmed scope overrides — Bangladesh and one brand

The owner's latest clarification takes precedence over the reference-site navigation:

- **Bangladesh only for the current release.** No country/region/market switcher, international storefront selection or geolocation-based country prompt. Use BDT. Checkout and saved delivery addresses use Bangladesh as the fixed supported country; district/city/area/postcode and local delivery rules remain necessary. Validate the supported country server-side rather than relying on a hidden selector.
- **One brand across all departments.** No Aarong/Taaga/Taaga Man-style brand tabs, sub-brand landing hierarchy, brand switcher or multi-brand filter. Use the owner's single brand identity, with men/women/kids/lifestyle and product categories underneath it. Do not require a brand selection when staff create products.
- These exclusions do not remove SKU variants, category/collection navigation, internal warehouses/stores, stock transfers or delivery addresses. Sub-brands and physical inventory locations are different concepts. Public store-finding remains conditional on actual publishable retail outlets; do not invent branches.
- Remaining Aarong-inspired design, interface and functional coverage stays in scope, together with the requested mixed image/video experience. Further country or sub-brand expansion requires a later explicit scope decision; do not build those selectors speculatively.

## Visual direction

The public store should closely follow the reference's shopping hierarchy: a single brand header and department/category navigation, without country or sub-brand selectors; campaign-led landing sections; generous white space; large consistent fashion photography; restrained borders; compact but readable product names/prices; unobtrusive wishlist/quick-view actions; filters close to results; and a gallery beside a clear purchase area on wide screens. Mobile must retain the same capabilities with touch-friendly drawers and controls.

The inspected listing declares Futura PT. Select an appropriately licensed font with comparable visual character when implementing the storefront; do not silently copy font files or introduce a paid font dependency. Preserve accessible contrast, readable sizing, visible focus and labels, and use the user's own brand identity, photography, video, copy and business policies. Reference branding, product records and corporate programmes are not the user's business content.

Staff operations should share the typography/spacing/colour discipline, predictable filters and reusable visual tokens. Its screens still need operational tables, stock locations, quantities, reasons, versions and permission-aware actions. Public shopping pages and staff operations use purpose-specific layouts over connected data. Do not infer Aarong's private ERP from its public website.

## Data and API changes needed

1. **Keep stable product/SKU identities.** Procurement, stock and future sales already reference them. New presentation data must be additive and must not rewrite posted history or reset the database.
2. **Separate specification from sellable options.** Fabric/care/fit/marketing content usually describe a style; size/colour combinations identify stock-bearing SKUs. Model typed product attributes and normalized filter vocabularies, with overrides only where variants genuinely differ. A filter must match an actually purchasable variant when several option filters are combined.
3. **Separate hierarchy from merchandising.** Keep a primary category, allow secondary category/collection membership, single-brand identity configuration, product-type metadata, season/occasion, curated related items and independently ordered menus/landing sections. A product can belong to several campaigns without duplicating stock. Categories and campaigns are not sub-brands; no multi-brand schema or staff brand picker is required for this release.
4. **Version public content.** Add slug/SEO fields, draft/preview/publish state, visibility dates and content completeness checks. Operational active/draft/archive state is not a substitute for an explicit publication policy. Review existing variant-count and payload limits when expanding content; use separate bounded resources instead of an unlimited product request.
5. **Publish an explicit safe projection.** Public DTOs must expose only permitted content, sellable prices and availability. They must never serialize supplier prices, staff notes, draft media, audit details, internal balances or originals merely because these are in a staff response. Publishing a media derivative must be an explicit action.
6. **Connect availability to inventory.** Physical on-hand is already recorded; reserved/sellable/quarantined quantities are not. Define reservations, expiry, safety stock and fulfillment rules before checkout. Store discovery must use the same SKU/location truth without leaking unrestricted internal quantities.
7. **Keep a separate public frontend boundary.** The staff React/Vite app remains a private operational interface. Evaluate a stable supported server-rendering approach for a storefront app when that slice begins, so crawlable product/category pages, metadata and first-load performance can be tested. No new framework is selected/installed just from a visual reference.
8. **Test migrations and ownership.** Add schema changes in reviewed migrations with compatibility for existing photos, products, suppliers, POs and ledger data. Share typed contracts/components where useful; do not expose staff APIs by removing authentication.

## Mixed image/video media contract

- Preserve uploaded masters unchanged, checksummed and downloadable by permitted staff, continuing the accepted original-image policy. Generate separate public display derivatives; do not force customers to download large masters.
- Add typed media records for image/video, product/optional variant association, sort order, alt text/title, poster, dimensions/duration, original metadata and processing/publication state. Existing image records must migrate compatibly, without losing original-download support.
- Validate actual container/codec/duration/dimensions, byte limits and processing concurrency before publication. Video limits are separate from the current image policy; the image-upload endpoint does not accept video today. Select and verify a maintained processing tool only when implementing it.
- Model pending/processing/ready/failed states; failed uploads do not produce broken published cards. Keep atomic metadata/audit and safe retry/cleanup semantics. New hosted storage/CDN/transcoding services require an explicit cost decision before adoption.
- Provide a mixed thumbnail rail, visible video indicator, poster fallback, play/pause/mute controls, keyboard navigation and captions when speech conveys product information. Pause inactive/off-screen videos, avoid sound autoplay and honour reduced-motion/data-saving needs. Lazy-load video and use metadata/posters initially.
- Make shoppable video cards optional managed content linked to the same product/variant IDs. No dependency on copying Tabaya's theme or a Shopify-only widget is implied.
